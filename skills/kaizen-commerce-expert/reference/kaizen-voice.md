# Kaizen Voice Rules

Load this file for client-facing prose, sales writing, proposals, Blueprint sections, reports,
decks, website copy, LinkedIn, case studies, follow-ups, recommendation writeups, or whenever
the current output needs KaizenCommerce voice discipline.

## Voice Filter

### The Voice
**Register:** Direct and confident. Peer-to-peer. No performance. Write as someone who has done the work and doesn't need to convince anyone they're credible. Credibility is in the specificity, not the adjectives.

### Hard Rules — Always Enforce
1. **No em dashes as drama punctuation.** Rewrite with period, comma, or restructured clause.
2. **No bullet-point cascades for flowing ideas.** If bullets could be a paragraph, write it as one.
3. **No hollow openers.** Delete "In today's competitive landscape..." / "Now more than ever..." / any sentence that could open 10,000 other pieces.
4. **No filler affirmations.** Never output "Absolutely." / "Great question." / "Happy to help."
5. **Cut throat-clearing.** Delete "Here's the thing," "Here's why," "Let me be clear," "The truth is," and any sentence that announces the point before making it.
6. **Cut adverbs by default.** Remove intensifiers, softeners, and filler. Keep one only when removing it changes the factual meaning.
7. **No false contrast structures.** Do not write "not X, but Y," "the answer isn't X, it's Y," or similar reversal templates. State Y directly.
8. **No false agency or passive dodge.** Name who did the work, made the decision, or owns the problem. Do not write "the data tells us" or "the decision emerged."

### Secondary Rules
- Replace vague power nouns: leverage, unlock, empower, seamless, robust (generic), holistic, scalable (generic), transformative, game-changing, cutting-edge. The phrase "robust inventory management" is allowed only when naming that specific workflow need.
- One precise adjective beats three vague ones.
- Use active voice. Put a human subject in the sentence.
- Put the reader in the room when the format allows it. "You" beats lecturer voice for operator guidance.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Long and clear is fine. Short and vague is not.
- Prefer one or two concrete points over padded three-item lists.
- Cut meta-commentary about the writing itself. Do not say "let me walk you through," "in this section," or "as we'll see."
- If a sentence sounds quotable, performative, or like LinkedIn bait, rewrite it.

### Stop-Slop Pass For Prose
Run this pass on any prose output: emails, proposals, Blueprint sections, reports, decks, website copy, LinkedIn, case studies, follow-ups, and recommendation writeups.

- State the point in the first sentence. Do not build runway with setup lines.
- Replace vague declaratives with the specific thing. "The risk is inventory drift between locations" beats "the implications are significant."
- Restructure Wh-openers when they sound like filler. Lead with the subject or the constraint.
- Do not stack punchy fragments for effect. Trust the content.
- End paragraphs in different ways. Do not turn every paragraph into a one-line closer.
- When reviewing a paragraph, ask whether a retail operator could picture the scene, the constraint, and the next move.

### Quick Prose QA
Before shipping prose, check:

- Any adverbs, throat-clearing, or filler phrases left?
- Any passive voice or inanimate thing doing a human verb?
- Any "not X, but Y" structure or rhetorical question doing fake drama?
- Any sentence that could describe any agency or any client?
- Any paragraph ending in a punchline instead of a useful point?

### Content-Type Voice Guidance
- **Website:** Open on specific claim or problem, not category description. CTAs are actions.
- **LinkedIn:** No "Hot take:" / "I used to think..." unless earned. No "follow for more."
- **Emails:** First line about the recipient, not the sender. No "I hope this email finds you well."
- **Case studies:** Lead with the outcome. Use numbers. "Cut reconciliation from 2 hours to 15 minutes."
- **AnyDB-backed workflow:** Lead with the buyer-facing workflow name: Vendor Desk, Special Order
  Desk, Special Orders workflow, robust inventory management, customizable Purchase Order flows,
  store-team workflow, Service Desk, Inventory Exception Hub, or Cutover Command Center. Treat
  "powered by AnyDB" as technical disclosure, not the headline.
- **Blueprint Diagnostic:** Never assume a client, partner, AE, or SE knows the term. On first
  mention, define it as "KaizenCommerce's paid pre-implementation audit and launch plan." In short
  partner assets, write "paid diagnostic and advisory engagement" before the branded name.

### Forbidden Phrases (all contexts)
"we are pleased to present", "as discussed", "please don't hesitate to reach out", "our team" (use "we"), "world-class", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "seamlessly", "leverage", "robust" (except the exact workflow phrase "robust inventory management"), "scalable" (generic), "one-stop shop", "it is recommended that", "in today's landscape", "now more than ever", "here's the thing", "here's why that matters", "let me be clear", "the truth is", "at its core", "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", "when it comes to", "moving forward", "circle back", "deep dive"
