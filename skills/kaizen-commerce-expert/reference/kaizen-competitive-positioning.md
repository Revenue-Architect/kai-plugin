# Kaizen Competitive Positioning — Agency Level

Load this reference when a deal has a named or implied competing path: another Shopify partner,
an enterprise SI, the merchant doing it themselves, or a POS vendor's in-house migration team.
This module positions **KaizenCommerce as the implementer**. Platform-level competition (Shopify
vs Square/Lightspeed/Clover) lives in `skills/kaizen-reference/kaizen-ref-competitive.md` — do
not restate it here.

**Provenance:** positioning logic and talk tracks below are firm strategy, not client results.
Any proof point used alongside them obeys the proof bank's Provenance & Capture Schema
(`reference/kaizen-proposal-proof-bank.md`) — `proposal-safe: yes` entries only in client-facing
language. Win/loss observations against each competitor type are flywheel targets: capture them
at `Close Client`.

## Rules (all competitor conversations)

- Never name-bash. Position on what we do differently, not on what they do badly.
- Never invent a competitor's price, timeline, or capability. If the merchant quotes one, treat
  it as merchant-stated evidence ("you mentioned their quote was lower") — never repeat it as fact
  elsewhere.
- Any claim about a competitor platform's current capability routes through the vendor-freshness
  protocol before it appears in a deliverable.
- If the honest answer is that the alternative fits better, say so and route to the Blueprint or
  a referral. Credibility compounds; one overreached deal poisons the AE channel.

## Segment 1 — vs. Generic Shopify Partners (theme/design agencies doing POS on the side)

**Their pull:** existing relationship, broad service menu, often cheaper headline quote.
**Structural weakness:** retail operations and migration are not their core practice. POS work
arrives bundled with web projects; reconciliation, parallel validation, and staff cutover
discipline are usually absent from their quotes — which is why the quotes look lighter.
**Talk track:** "Most Shopify agencies are excellent at storefronts. Multi-location retail
operations is a different discipline — opening inventory counts, gift card liability, day-one
reconciliation. Ask any agency you're considering how they validate inventory accuracy at
cutover. That answer tells you everything."
**Wedge questions for discovery:** Who on their team has run a multi-location POS cutover? What
happens to your gift card balances? What does their parallel-run period look like?
**When we lose honestly:** storefront-led projects where POS is incidental — refer or partner
rather than stretch.

## Segment 2 — vs. Enterprise SIs and consultancies

**Their pull:** procurement comfort, governance optics, staffing depth, existing MSAs.
**Structural weakness:** cost and pace. Multi-location specialty retail at the 2–20 location
scale is below their economics; the work lands with junior staff on long timelines.
**Talk track:** "You'd be a small account there. Here you are the practice. The people who scope
your migration are the people who run it — and the diagnostic is a fixed-fee deliverable you own
in two weeks, not a discovery phase that bills until it finds something."
**Proof posture:** speed-to-value and senior attention; the Blueprint is the proof, not a slide
about it.
**When we lose honestly:** global rollouts, procurement-mandated vendor lists, >50-location
enterprises needing 24/7 SLAs we don't carry. Say so early; stay the diagnostic layer.

## Segment 3 — vs. DIY (merchant self-serve or generalist freelancer)

**Their pull:** lowest visible cost; Shopify's own docs make it look turnkey.
**Structural truth to respect:** for a simple single-location store with clean data and no gift
cards, DIY is genuinely viable — say that. The risk curve bends at multi-location, gift card
liability, historical data, and staff cutover.
**Talk track:** "Plenty of merchants self-migrate successfully — usually one location, clean
catalog, no gift cards. At your scale the failure mode isn't 'it doesn't work,' it's three weeks
of inventory you can't trust while trading. The Blueprint Diagnostic, KaizenCommerce's paid
pre-implementation audit and launch plan, exists so you can decide with the risks mapped —
including the DIY path. You own the report either way."
**Advisory wedge:** when the merchant has a strong technical team, do not force a delivery pitch.
Position Blueprint Diagnostic + Advisory as the lower-risk path: KaizenCommerce guides launch
architecture, workflow translation, QA readiness, rollout planning, and the real implementation
number while the merchant team owns the build.
**Full implementation handoff:** if the merchant needs Shopify POS delivery, existing tech stack
integrations, Special Orders workflow, robust inventory management, customizable Purchase Order
flows, or store-team workflow, route to full implementation instead of pure advisory.
**Risk framing (no invented numbers):** name the specific irreversibles — gift card balances,
order history, opening counts — and who owns the failure if they break.
**When we lose honestly:** if the Blueprint shows DIY is safe for them, recommend it. That
referral becomes the reference that wins the next five deals.

## Segment 4 — vs. POS-vendor professional services (Lightspeed/Square in-house teams)

**Their pull:** bundled "free" or cheap migration with the software contract.
**Structural weakness:** scope is data transfer, not operations. Vendor PS migrates rows; nobody
owns the operating model, the back-office workflows, or the question of whether the vendor's own
platform is the right target.
**Talk track:** "Their migration team's job is to get you onto their platform. Ours is to get
your operation working — which starts with whether that platform is even the right call. Those
are different services with different incentives."

## Pricing-Rationale Narratives (risk transferred, not deliverables listed)

Use these narrative frames in proposals and live conversations. Figures always from
`reference/kaizen-pricing.md` — this section supplies the *why*, never the number.

| Element | Risk transferred to KaizenCommerce |
|---|---|
| Blueprint (fixed fee, credited) | Scoping risk — the paid pre-implementation audit and launch plan shows the real scope before committing, and the fee isn't sunk |
| Fixed tier fee | Budget risk — overruns inside scope are ours, not change-ordered surprises |
| Buyer-facing rollout plan | Continuity risk — workflow and data are proven before broader rollout |
| Warranty window (per pricing canon) | Defect risk — our implementation errors are our cost, in writing |
| Retainer attach | Drift risk — the system keeps matching the operation after go-live |

One-sentence close: "The fee isn't for hours — it's for who owns the failure modes."

## Objection Handling (AECR: Acknowledge, Empathize, Clarify, Reframe)

- **"We have a cheaper quote."** Clarify what the quote includes: parallel validation? gift card
  reconciliation? training sign-off? warranty in writing? Reframe: the delta is usually the risk
  transfer, not margin.
- **"Shopify referred us to another partner too."** Normal — AEs refer multiple partners. Offer
  the wedge questions from Segment 1 as the comparison rubric; we win on the answers.
- **"Our IT person can handle it."** Respect it; route to Segment 3 framing. IT competence isn't
  the gap — retail cutover experience is.
- **"A bigger firm feels safer."** Segment 2 framing: ask who exactly will run their account and
  what the diagnostic costs there.
