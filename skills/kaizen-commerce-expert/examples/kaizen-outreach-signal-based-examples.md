# Kaizen Signal-Based Outreach Examples

These are synthetic examples for Kai quality training. They are not Kaizen client artifacts.

## Example 1: Generic Outreach

Weak email:

```text
Subject: Shopify POS help

Hi Sarah,

I hope you are well. I wanted to check in and see if you are interested in improving your retail
operations with Shopify POS. KaizenCommerce helps retailers streamline operations and scale.

Would you be open to a call?
```

Why it fails:

- no signal
- generic value claim
- "checking in" framing
- no operational reframe
- could go to any retailer

Strong email:

```text
Subject: new store inventory strain

Hi Sarah,

Saw you are hiring for the new West Island location. The risk I usually see at that point is not
store setup itself. It is inventory truth splitting across locations, ecommerce, and staff
workarounds before the new team has a stable rhythm.

Kaizen helps retailers pressure-test that operating layer before a POS move or rollout: location
mapping, inventory ownership, staff workflows, and cutover risk.

Worth a 15-minute diagnostic next week?
```

Why it works:

- starts from a timing signal
- teaches an operational risk
- does not overclaim
- one specific ask

## Example 2: Partner/AE Outreach

Weak message:

```text
Do you have any retailers who need Shopify POS help? We can support migrations and implementation.
```

Why it fails:

- too broad
- no trigger for referral
- no partner usefulness

Strong message:

```text
If you see a multi-location retailer where inventory trust is the blocker, that is a strong Kaizen
fit.

The signal is usually specific: staff calling other stores to confirm stock, ecommerce orders being
cancelled after purchase, or managers rebuilding inventory truth in spreadsheets before buying
decisions.

For those accounts, we can start with a focused Blueprint instead of jumping straight to a migration
quote. It gives the merchant a cleaner decision and gives the AE a safer implementation path.
```

Why it works:

- gives partner a referral pattern
- explains when Kaizen fits
- keeps the two-lane, scope-first commercial model

## Example 3: Follow-Up With New Signal

Weak follow-up:

```text
Just checking in to see if you had thoughts on my last email.
```

Why it fails:

- no new value
- creates no reason to respond

Strong follow-up:

```text
One more thought on the inventory issue you mentioned.

If the Friday count is the only moment the team trusts stock, the real problem is not just system
accuracy. It is that no one can see where trust breaks during the week: receiving, transfers,
returns, or online orders.

That is exactly what I would map in a Blueprint before recommending a migration path.
```

Why it works:

- adds a useful reframe
- connects to their stated pain
- does not push for a meeting without value
