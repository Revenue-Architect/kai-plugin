# Good vs Bad: Proposal Situation Section

Use when writing or reviewing Section 2 of a proposal.

## Bad

"Your business is growing and needs a scalable commerce platform that can streamline operations
and unlock better customer experiences. KaizenCommerce will help modernize your retail stack
with Shopify POS and best-in-class migration support."

## Why It Fails

- Starts with generic business growth language
- Uses banned or weak phrases
- Does not prove Kaizen understands the merchant's reality
- No reason for urgency
- No bridge to scope or price

## Good

"Celi's team is not looking for a prettier POS screen. They need a retail system that can keep
online and in-store inventory aligned across active selling days without forcing staff to rebuild
trust manually each morning.

The current WooCommerce and in-store setup creates two separate versions of the business. Products
can be sold online before store-level availability is clean, customer history is split between
channels, and reporting requires the team to reconcile exports instead of reading one operating
view. That is manageable when volume is low. It becomes expensive when the team is trying to make
buying, staffing, and store decisions with stale information.

This proposal scopes the next step as a controlled Shopify implementation, not a platform swap
done in one jump. The work starts with data and workflow validation, then moves through migration,
configuration, staff training, and parallel validation before cutover."

## Why It Works

- Opens on the merchant's actual operating problem
- Names the split between systems and decision-making impact
- Makes the implementation approach feel controlled
- Avoids hype and generic agency language
